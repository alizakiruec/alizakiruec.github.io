Ali Zakir

JSPS Postdoctoral Fellow · Sony Computer Science Laboratories, Inc.
PhD in Informatics · University of Electro-Communications, Tokyo

I study how the body moves and what it reveals. My work sits at the intersection of computer vision and behavioral neuroscience — building systems that can read human pose, motion, and physiological signals together, to infer states that neither modality could decode alone. The long-term goal is AI that understands people not just from what they say, but from how they move, react, and physically respond to the world around them.

Research

Human & Hand Pose Estimation

Designing efficient, attention-driven architectures for accurate 2D pose estimation under real-world conditions — occlusion, scale variation, and resource constraints.

Multimodal Behavior Analysis

Fusing video, motion features, and physiological signals (EDA, ECG) to decode emotional and cognitive states — stress, anxiety, attention — that visual data alone cannot reliably identify.

Biosignal & Video Synchronization

Developing reproducible pipelines that temporally align wearable sensor streams with multi-camera video for behavioral neuroscience applications.

Multi-Person Motion Analysis

Tracking and characterizing groups of people in unconstrained environments — extracting spatiotemporal motion features for downstream behavioral modeling.

Biomedical AI

Prior work on brain tumor classification, Alzheimer's identification, cancer peptide discrimination, and protein prediction using deep learning and graph-based methods.

Automatic Speech Recognition

Early research on isolated digit recognition for the Pashto language using MFCC features — a low-resource language with limited prior computational work.

About

I grew up in Peshawar, Pakistan — a region with rich languages and limited computational resources. My early research on Pashto speech recognition came directly from that context: a language spoken by tens of millions of people with almost no digital infrastructure. That instinct — to work on problems that matter to communities that are underserved by mainstream AI research — has stayed with me.

My PhD at the University of Electro-Communications in Tokyo focused on making pose estimation smarter and lighter — more attention-aware, more efficient, and better at handling the messy real-world conditions that benchmarks don't capture. As a JSPS Fellow at Sony CSL, that work has expanded outward: from single-body pose to groups of people, from vision alone to vision plus physiological signals, from static benchmarks to live behavioral data.

Outside research, I am a reviewer for the International Journal of Speech Technology.
